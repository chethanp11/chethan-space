Human-in-the-loop should not be a vague safety phrase. It should be a concrete workflow design. Adding a human reviewer at the end of an AI process does not automatically create accountability. The review must be structured, informed, and connected to clear decision rights.

## What good review includes

Reviewers need context, source evidence, confidence signals, known limitations, and clear choices. They also need to know what they are accountable for approving. Are they reviewing accuracy, policy compliance, tone, business judgment, or final release? Each requires different information.

## Design questions

A useful human-in-the-loop workflow answers:

- What can the system do automatically?
- What requires human judgment?
- What evidence must be shown?
- What are the reviewer choices?
- When should work be escalated?
- How are review decisions logged?

## Avoid false control

Human review can become a false control if the reviewer lacks time, context, or authority. In that case, the workflow creates the appearance of governance without real oversight. Good design reduces cognitive burden and highlights what matters most.

## Review states matter

Simple states such as draft, reviewed, approved, rejected, escalated, and archived can make accountability visible. They also help teams learn from review patterns. If many outputs are rejected for the same reason, the system should improve.

## The outcome

Human review adds value when it is intentional. It should improve quality, preserve accountability, and create feedback loops. In enterprise AI, the goal is not to replace judgment. The goal is to focus human judgment where it matters most.
